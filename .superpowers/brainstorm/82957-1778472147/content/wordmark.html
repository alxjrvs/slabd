Wordmark — three directions

All set in Oswald (condensed sans). Each direction shows the wordmark (header / marketing use) and the app icon (Home-screen, 64×64) together — they need to read as one system. Palette is Catalog Index: warm-white / deep-ink / vermilion / muted-gold.

A · Pure Type

Wordmark: "SLABD" in Oswald, no marks, no enclosure.
App icon: Single S on deep ink, rounded square.
Says: "We don't need a logo, the name is the logo." Quiet, confident.
Trade-off: Lowest distinctiveness — the work is all in the type. App icon is also the most generic of the three (every fintech starts with an S).

B · Slab Bracket

Wordmark: "SLABD" with two vertical bars — left in ink, right in vermilion. The bars are the slab's edges.
App icon: Letter S encased between the same two bars. Reads as a tiny graded slab from across the room.
Says: "The thing in the case is the brand." Literal encapsulation iconography.
Trade-off: Slightly literal — depends on whether the slab metaphor lands fast.

C · Stamp Seal

Wordmark: "SLABD" inside a rectangular stamp with "Cert. No." top + "Est. MMXXVI" bottom. Reads like a slab label.
App icon: Gold stamped S, slightly off-axis (as if pressed). Authentication coding.
Says: "This is officially graded." Heritage / certifying-body voice.
Trade-off: Most "earned trust" feel, but also most institutional — might read older than the brand wants.

System note: whichever you pick, the wordmark and app icon must read as siblings. A is the safest production-wise (no custom drawing). B is the most ownable visually (the slab bars become a brand element you can extend — section dividers, button shapes, etc.). C is the most "authenticated" but hardest to keep contemporary.
