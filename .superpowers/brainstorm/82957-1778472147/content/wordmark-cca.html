App icon — Comics Code Authority parody

The CCA seal (1954–2011) is one of the most recognizable marks ever printed on a comic cover — circular, "APPROVED BY THE COMICS CODE AUTHORITY" around the rim, big stylized "A" in the center. Older sellers will read it instantly; younger collectors recognize it from reprints. Pure in-group iconography for a comics marketplace.

Three takes. Wordmark across all three: "SLABD" in Oswald (Pure Type from the previous screen — quiet, lets the seal carry the personality).

Faithful CCA parody.
"APPROVED BY THE SLABD COMICS MARKETPLACE" around the rim, big S in center.

A · Faithful Parody

Reads as: Direct CCA homage — older collectors clock it in under a second.
Says: "This is the comics-buying app." Maximum in-group signal.
Trade-off: At 64×64 the rim text is too small to read — works as visual texture only. Center S carries the recognition at small sizes.

B · Modernized Seal

Reads as: Inherits the CCA silhouette without copying the typography. Reads correctly at any size — big paper-S on dark seal.
Says: "Heritage stamp, contemporary execution."
Trade-off: Loses some homage-charm in exchange for legibility. Best long-haul choice for an app icon (Home-screen size).

Slight rotation + roughened edge = "just pressed onto the cover."

C · Stamped Impression

Reads as: The seal as a physical rubber-stamp impression in red ink on the cover. Strongest tactile / archival feel.
Says: "Hand-approved." Antithesis of generic SaaS.
Trade-off: The roughened edge is charming but adds visual noise at icon sizes. The Apple/Google icon guidelines push toward cleaner silhouettes — this works better in marketing than as a Home-screen icon. Could be used alongside a cleaner app icon (option A or B for OS, C for marketing/social).

Note on the scope correction: the rim text on all three reads "Marketplace for Comics" — not "Graded Comics" — to match the broader scope. The slab in "Slabd" stays as a name, not a literal product constraint. Letterboxd isn't about literal boxes either.
